# Functional-Dark-Matter---NMPFs

Microbial Dark Matter (MDM) encompasses a wide range of uncultivated microorganisms found on Earth. These elusive microbes have not been successfully cultured in a lab environment, posing difficulties for traditional study methods. Previously thought to be nonviable, microbiologists and bioinformaticians now turn to cultivation-independent techniques like metagenomics and metatranscriptomics to investigate this enigmatic realm [1]. In the last couple of decades, there have been groundbreaking advancements in DNA sequencing and computational tools. Recently, investigations into Microbial Dark Matter (MDM) have relied on culture-independent techniques to retrieve genetic material. These methods encompass unicellular genomics and shotgun metagenomics, facilitating the creation of Single-Amplified Genomes (SAGs) and Metagenome-Assembled Genomes (MAGs). These avenues shed light on the evolutionary paths and metabolic processes of MDM. Despite significant strides forward, the functional diversity of MDM remains largely unexplored [2]. MDM contains unique genes, pathways, and functions that hold untapped potential, including insights into human health and the possibility of uncovering new antibiotics and other biotechnological uses. Additionally, MDM presents an opportunity for AI techniques to enhance our understanding of microbial communities [3].
